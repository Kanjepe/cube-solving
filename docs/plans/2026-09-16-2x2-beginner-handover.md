## 2. The owner's method (source of truth)

Stated by the owner on 2026-09-16, in his words translated; "mala" = the two stickers of one side that belong to one layer (a same-colour pair), "perpendikulāri" = diagonal.

1. White layer, corner by corner: `R U R' U'` (white down, target corner up-front-right, repeat until placed).
2. Yellow face: `R' D' R D` as in the guide (he can also use Sune `R U R' U R U2 R'`; he said the guide's `R' D' R D` is longer but simpler and the same as the 3x3 last-layer corners, keep it).
3. "Malas vienā krāsā": with white down and yellow up, look at each side. If one layer has a side pair of one colour ("gatava mala"), hold the OTHER face (yellow or white) in FRONT with that pair at the bottom-back, run `R' U R' D2 R U' R' D2 R2` once. Result: that whole layer's four side pairs are correct, both faces still one colour.
4. Corners of the remaining layer: "blakus" (adjacent) -> `L' U R' D2 R U' R' D2 R2`; "pretī / perpendikulāri" (diagonal) -> `R U' R' U' F2 U' R U R' D R2`.

He says these always finish the cube.

## 3. Simulator facts (all reproducible with `_build/verify_algs.py`, corners only)

- Step 3 algorithm `R' U R' D2 R U' R' D2 R2`: a 3-cycle of the BACK layer corners (DBL <- UBR, DBR <- DBL, UBR <- DBR; UBL fixed). The FRONT layer is untouched. With yellow in front and white at the back (or the reverse), both faces stay one colour. For every one of the 16 "exactly one pair" permutations of a layer, holding that layer at the back with the pair on the bottom and running the algorithm once leaves that layer with all four side pairs correct (up to a turn of that layer). Verified: `_build/test_guide2_solves.py::test_a_perm_finishes_every_one_pair_layer_and_keeps_both_faces`.
- Step 4 adjacent algorithm `L' U R' D2 R U' R' D2 R2`: EARLIER TODAY I WRONGLY REPORTED THIS AS BROKEN. It is the same A perm performed on the TOP layer, and the cube ends rotated by `x'` in the hands (because on a 2x2, `L' = R' x`). Checked: after the algorithm plus a whole-cube `x'`, the bottom layer is intact, the top is oriented and three top corners are cycled (BL <- FR, BR <- BL, FR <- BR). My first analysis compared absolute positions and mistook the final whole-cube rotation for "seven corners moved and twisted". The case it solves: solved layer DOWN, unsolved layer UP, its single same-colour pair at the BACK (top row of the back face), then run the algorithm. This still needs one confirmation on a real cube with the owner: which side he puts the pair on.
- Diagonal algorithm `R U' R' U' F2 U' R U R' D R2`: with the diagonal layer up (yellow up, white down) it works from any front and any U offset; the bottom layer may come out rotated (align with U afterwards). Verified in `test_guide2_solves.py`.
- Whole method: all 576 states (both faces oriented, any permutation of both layers) and all 648 last-layer states finish with steps 3+4 as modelled in `arrange()` in `test_guide2_solves.py`.
